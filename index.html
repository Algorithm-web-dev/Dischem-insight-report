Executive summary

Three things to take into the room

All figures come from Dis-Chem's GA4 property. The “before” window is 14 Jun – 17 Aug 2026 and the “after” window is 18 Aug – 21 Oct 2026, 65 days each, starting where the last report's data ended.

1

The mobile checkout fix is working

Mobile begin checkout → purchase is up +4.0 pts (36.9% to 40.9%). Weekly data shows a lasting step change from the week of 23 Aug, and mobile revenue per week is up +33% against +19% on desktop. Desktop is not confirmed: its whole-journey rate slipped (62.3% to 58.1%).

2

The average hides two stories

Revenue per session is up only +1.8% overall (R4.65 to R4.73). It rose +20% in Cross-network, +22% in Direct and +27% in Organic Search, and by +7.4% excluding Paid Social. Without the promotion weeks, the daily revenue lift is +10.6%.

3

Paid Social doubled and barely converts

Paid Social sessions per day are up +111%. It is now 11.4% of sessions but 0.5% of revenue, and its purchase rate is 0.03% against 0.78% for Direct. The share of users who view a product fell from 54.6% to 49.4%, which fits this shift. This is why overall conversion looks flat even though checkout improved.

Fix or promotion? Why we think the fix is realGA4 places the mobile checkout fix in the week of 23 Aug, and the 25 Aug – 14 Sep promotion started two days later, so revenue growth in that stretch cannot be split between the two. Checkout completion can be separated, because a promotion brings more shoppers but does not normally change how many checkout starters finish. Three things point to the fix:

1. It stepped once and stayed. Purchases per mobile checkout starter averaged 35.6% in the ten weeks before the fix and 39.1% in the eight weeks from the week of 23 Aug (weekly figures, so slightly different from the 36.9% to 40.9% 65-day view, but the same direction and size). They averaged 38.8% in the three promotion weeks and 39.3% in the five weeks after, so they did not fall back when the promotion ended.
2. No promotion bump in checkout. Shipping → payment rose from 50% to 54% and held at the same level inside and outside the promotion weeks. By device, the gain at this step is on iPhone (50.2% to 56.7%) while Android slipped slightly (52.3% to 50.4%). Purchases per add-to-cart user still rose on both (27% on iPhone, 30% on Android), so the fix shows on both devices overall.
3. It is mobile only. Desktop did not show the gain, and a site-wide promotion would be expected to lift both.

How much is the fix worth? Purchases per mobile checkout starter rose about 10%. If the number of starters had been unchanged, that is roughly R3.1M of extra revenue over 23 Aug – 17 Oct, about 6% of revenue in that period. Using different before-periods as the baseline gives a range of R2.5M to R3.4M (5% to 6% of revenue). This is an estimate, not a measurement. It also assumes the number of checkout starters would have been the same and that the fix did not change how checkout events are recorded. The rest of the growth (+22.9% overall, +10.6% without the promotion weeks) comes from extra traffic, the promotion and other factors. We also have no deployment records, so we cannot rule out another site change in the same week.

What stands out

The promotion weeks lift the headlineRevenue peaked at R3.6M on 01 Sep. The first 28 days averaged R1,017K a day and the last 28 averaged R859K (-15.5%).
The 4–10 Oct dip is a return to baselineGA4 flagged revenue -28.9% for 4–10 Oct against the week before (R4.98M vs R7.00M). That comparison week was inflated: 27 Sep was a R1.9M day and the rest of the week averaged R843K. 4–10 Oct averaged R711K a day, in line with the R690K mid-month average before the promotions (+3.1%). In the before window, revenue from the 25th to the 3rd ran about 27% above the rest of the month (R873K a day), which fits a salary cycle, although it rests on two month-ends. The lowest day, 10 Oct (R419K), was a Saturday, our weakest day (typically R466K). We read this as a return to baseline and not a post-promotion decline. The data does not say what drove the 27 Sep spike, but that does not change the conclusion: the dip week sits at the normal mid-month level.

Read this carefully before quoting the checkout numbersThe two views disagree about the size of the gain. On the whole-journey view, mobile begin checkout → purchase went from 36.9% to 40.9%. On the step-by-step view it went from 26.9% to 35.8%. The checkout journey counted 37,196 purchasers before against 43,754 on the purchase journey, and the two now agree (52,975 and 51,935). Part of the jump in the step view is probably shipping and payment events being recorded properly now. The conservative figure is the improvement on the whole-journey view. The same applies to desktop: its step view shows a gain, but the whole-journey view shows begin checkout → purchase slipping (62.3% to 58.1%), so we do not claim a desktop improvement. In the weekly data, add payment users and purchase users are almost identical, so the payment → purchase step is the least reliable number here. We rely on shipping → payment and begin checkout → purchase.
Shipping → payment is still the biggest dropAbout 46% of mobile users who add shipping do not reach payment. This is now the largest remaining loss inside checkout, and it is sized in the CRO Report.

Recommendations

What we'd do first

These are the diagnostic and acquisition actions that come out of the data. Conversion optimisation work is in the CRO Report.

P1

Fix the 902 Merchant Center products

Correct the “Invalid sale price” configuration so the products return to Google Search and Shopping.

Why: free traffic is being lost now, and the fix is contained.
P1

Validate checkout event tracking

Confirm shipping, payment and purchase events fire consistently on every device and in both periods.

Why: the two checkout views disagree, so the size of the improvement is not certain until this is confirmed.
P2

Scope a repeat-purchase workstream

About two in three daily purchasers are first-time buyers. Treat retention as a separate CRM and lifecycle workstream.

Why: the loyalty opportunity is large, but it needs separate CRM data.
Optimisation items are in the October CRO ReportThe mobile shipping step, the cart-to-checkout step, landing and entry-point tests and the remaining payment-step loss are sized in the CRO Report, which also sets out the actions for this month and the metrics we will monitor until the November report. This report only covers what happened, why, and the data we still need.

Open data points

What this report does not yet show

The fix date is estimated, not confirmed. Weekly GA4 data places it in the week of 23 Aug, five days after our 18 Aug split, so the windows are close to clean.

Revenue by device is by week, not by day. It is split at whole GA4 weeks, so the windows are 10 and 8 weeks and the figures are weekly averages that do not match the 65-day totals above exactly.

Promotions and seasonality. The after window includes the 25 Aug – 14 Sep promotion stretch, and revenue follows a month-end pattern. The promotion dates are read from the revenue pattern, and the 27 Sep spike has no confirmed cause.

Channel data has limits. The channel windows differ by one day (66 and 65 days, so we compare per day), purchase rate by channel is only available for the after window, and there is no ad spend, so efficiency cannot be judged yet.

No session recordings in this report. The behavioural evidence from Clarity has not been refreshed since the last report.
